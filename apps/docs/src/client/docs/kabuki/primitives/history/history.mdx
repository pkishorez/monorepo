You may have noticed the timeline under every demo on this site. You can
drag it back, and the app goes back with it. This page is about how that
works. The short answer: you get it for free.

## Every Message is kept

Remember the rule from the start: the only way anything changes is a
Message. So if kabuki keeps every Message, in order, it has the whole story
of your app. That story is the **History**.

<Demo caption="Each Painted Message adds a dot. The ring goes back to Step 2, and the sketch is drawn as it was there.">
  <TravelPicture />
</Demo>

You don't write anything to get it. The sketch pad below is an Actor like
any other, with a Model, two Messages and two Updates. kabuki keeps its
Messages on its own.

## Going back is playing again

Updates are pure. The same Model and the same Message always give the same
answer. So to see the app as it was after any Message, kabuki starts from
`init` and plays the Messages again, up to that point. That's a **Replay**.

Showing a past moment this way is **Time Travel**. The live app keeps
running underneath, and the past is read only: you can look, but nothing
can be sent from there.

This is why Workers live outside Updates. A Replay plays only Messages and
Updates, so no request is sent twice and no timer fires twice.

## Carrying on from the past

You can also go back and carry on from there. The new Messages grow a new
**Branch** beside the old one, like a branch in git. Nothing is lost.

<Demo caption="Back at Step 2, the app carries on. A new line grows beside the old one, and the old one stays.">
  <BranchPicture />
</Demo>

## Saving is easy, too

Since the app is its Messages, saving the app is saving its History.
`history.save()` gives plain JSON. Hand it back with `toReact(…, { saved
})`, and the app carries on from where it was, even after a reload.

The History is yours to read, too. `App.useRuntime()` gives you the
Messages, a way to show any moment, and a way to carry on from one. The
timeline on this site is built on just that.
